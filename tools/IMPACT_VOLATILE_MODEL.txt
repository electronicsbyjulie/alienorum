EXCESS BOMBARDMENT AND SECONDARY VOLATILES

Scope
=====
The secondary atmosphere/water budget uses the same belt-proximity factor as
procedural crater generation. This is a scenario model, not a measured impact
flux. The supplied pressure and water-level overrides retain their authority.
Gas/ice giants still use their existing cloud-reference treatment.

Shared belt factor: B = clamp(sqrt(30 AU / inner belt radius), 1, 4).
Absent or unusable belt geometry selects B=1. The atmospheric suppression used
to draw fewer craters is not used in the volatile calculation.

The Solar-reference impact history is already absorbed into the previous
Solar-scale calibration. Only B-1 adds explicit late bombardment. Thus Solar
controls and systems without a closer known belt retain the calibrated budget.

Adopted numerical scenario
==========================
Incoming impactor mass / planet mass:
  min(0.01, 1e-5 * (B-1) * impact_flux_scale * age_gyr / 4.5).
This constant excess flux is a chosen normalization. The one-percent cap bounds
the approximation that the planet's mass and radius remain fixed.

Approach speed is the host-orbit circular speed sqrt(G Mstar / a); moons use
their parent body's stellar orbit. Let q = approach speed / escape speed.
Surface impact speed / escape speed is sqrt(1+q^2), including focusing.
The comet-like approach-speed choice follows the scenario in Wyatt, Kral and
Sinclair, https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10731, section 3.5.1. The outcome functions
below are heuristic coefficients, not that paper's published fitted prescription.

Retained fraction of incoming material: 1/(1+q^2).
Erosion mass capacity / incoming impactor mass: min(1, 0.03*q^2).
Defaults for arriving mass are 10% H2O, 1% CO2-equivalent, and 0.1% N2-equivalent;
the remainder is outside the tracked volatile species. This describes a mixed
icy/rubble population on comet-like trajectories, not a universal comet mixture.

Reservoir evolution
===================
1. Preserve the original mantle-release and stellar-escape calculation.
2. Separate retained H2O into surface-accessible and deep/interior storage.
3. Estimate phase exposure at an intermediate pool containing original retained
   volatiles plus half of the captured late delivery. This avoids treating an
   initially empty atmosphere as incapable of retaining any delivered gases.
4. Assign 90% of erosion capacity to atmospheric gas and 10% to mobile surface
   condensates. Each turnover dose is its capacity divided by the respective
   exposed pool. Weathered carbon storage and deep H2O remain protected from
   this excess-impact term. Their pre-existing stellar-escape model is unchanged.
5. Original exposed material survives a turnover dose d by exp(-d). Continuous
   incoming delivery uses (1-exp(-d))/d instead, accounting for its distribution
   of arrival times. Newly delivered gases also face the existing stellar loss
   rate; water uses the existing steam-sensitive loss proxy.
6. Recalculate weathering and condensation from the resulting pools. Delivery
   can increase water/ice storage even while atmospheric nitrogen is eroded.
   Vaporization alone is not counted as permanent escape.

For every species:
  initial + delivered = mantle + atmosphere + surface_storage + escaped.
The existing surface_storage ledger includes non-atmospheric retained material;
water_partition separately identifies its protected interior and accessible
condensed subdivisions. Incoming material lost immediately or subsequently is
included in escaped, with both contributions visible in impacts.species.
The pressure ceiling changes rendering pressure, not conservation accounting.

Scenario controls and persistence
=================================
Inventory fields impact_flux_scale, impactor_water_fraction,
impactor_co2_fraction, and impactor_nitrogen_fraction are serialized and validated.
Setting impact_flux_scale to zero recovers the prior calibrated scenario.
Zero initial inventory still permits incoming delivery; zero impactor volatile
fractions select dry bombardment. Species sums cannot exceed incoming mass.

The volatile-budget cache version is v4. The host's belt presence and radius
are part of its input key. Star saves retain debris_belt.present and
debris_belt.inner_edge_m; uncertain numerical annotations use the supplied value.
Legacy default inventories are normalized before deciding whether they were
edited, so added controls do not freeze an old default into a custom scenario.

Limits
======
No explicit impactor size spectrum, scattering dynamics, time-dependent belt
depletion, giant-impact stochasticity, impact heat clock, or biological damage
is simulated. Intermediate phase exposure is an approximation, not a coupled
time integration. The belt factor describes proximity, not total belt mass.
The numerical constants are exposed assumptions rather than confidence bounds.

Validation example
===================
A controlled Tau Ceti f scenario uses host mass 0.783 Solar, temperature 5344 K,
absolute visual magnitude 5.6885, [Fe/H] -0.52, age 7.63 Gyr, belt radius 1.5e12 m,
and a planet of 6.870596 Earth masses, 1.7 Earth radii, a=1.334 AU, e=0.16.
Other stellar abundance ratios are unspecified in this fixture. This isolates
the added term; a loaded world's additional inputs can give different values.

The belt factor is 1.72973; arriving impactors total 1.23729e-5 planet masses.
Pressure changes from 14.72605 to 12.79977 bar (-13.08%). Surface condensed water
changes from 4.8792464e-4 to 4.8851061e-4 planet masses (+0.1201%). Protected deep
water remains 2.5578787e-3 planet masses. In this case impacts erode atmospheric
nitrogen while retained icy delivery slightly outweighs surface-water erosion.

All 146 planet tests pass, including Solar controls, dry erosion, delivery onto
an initially empty world, conservation across temperature/mass/age regimes,
authored-value preservation, budget invalidation, and ordinary/compact saves.
All ten selected stellar-orbit catalog tests pass, including reading the actual
Tau Ceti belt record and observing an active impact term in its planet budget.
