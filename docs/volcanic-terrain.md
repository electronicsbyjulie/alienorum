# A volcanic surface for a tidally heated rocky world

## What the observations resolve

JWST measured the light from TRAPPIST-1 b over an orbit. Its dayside brightness temperature at 15 µm was 490 ± 17 K. The nightside signal and the longitudinal offset of the hottest hemisphere were consistent with zero. Fresh ultramafic rock fits the eclipse colors and gives a plausible source of a subdued olive tint. Disk-integrated infrared measurements leave individual eruptions, mineral abundances and visible colors unresolved. [Gillon et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41550-026-02806-9) · [Ducrot et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41550-024-02428-z)

The Io geologic map distinguishes plains across 65.8% of the surface, flow fields across 28.5%, and caldera floors across 2.5%. Diffuse material overlies about 18% of the surface. Many of the conspicuous white and red deposits owe their colors to sulfur dioxide and sulfur. Magellan supplies a comparison at roughly terrestrial gravity. At Mylitta Fluctus, several flows emerged from a roughly 325 km wide shield and streamed down into a plain, together covering a region about 800 by 380 km. These observations motivate drawing overlapping geological units with different ages, directions and scales. [Io geologic map](https://pubs.usgs.gov/sim/3168/sim3168_pamphlet.pdf) · [Head et al., Magellan observations](https://www.lpi.usra.edu/meetings/lpsc1991/pdf/1273.pdf)

## How the generator chooses a map

| Quantity | Calculation and interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| Available heat | The orbit supplies mean motion, eccentricity, primary mass and separation. In the synchronous, small-eccentricity limit, surface heat flux is $F=\frac{21}{8\pi}(k_2/Q)GM_*^2R^3ne^2/a^6$. The app uses the full eccentricity and spin expression. Dissipation is an interior assumption. In the models of Bolmont et al., changing the allowed eccentricity of b moves the nominal small-core estimate from 0.5 to about 500 W/m². [Interior models and Table 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03408) |
| Districts and locations | Above the display threshold of 0.2 W/m², activity is $A=\min(1,\ln(1+F)/\ln(11))$ with $F$ in W/m². The number of large districts is $4\pi R^2 A/(3\times10^{12}\,\mathrm{m^2})$, rounded down and bounded to 2–420. Each hosts one to six mapped centers, some strung out along a rift. Density, clustering and the activity scale are scenario choices. Uniform longitude and uniform sine of latitude give each unit of area equal prior weight on either hemisphere. Juno has detected polar hot spots, and the interior dissipation pattern of b is unknown. [Pettine et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05717) |
| Shape and relief | A log-uniform draw from 22–330 km supplies the edifice radius, capped at 0.095 planetary radii on small bodies. The short-to-long axis ratio spans 0.38–1.0. The pit occupies 10–45% of the edifice radius. An irregular rim surrounds a collapsed floor, sometimes interrupted by a crustal island. Height follows $H=\min(r\tan s,Y/(\rho g))$, with flank gradient 0.006–0.038, rock density 3,000 kg/m³ and an assumed 200 MPa limiting stress. These distributions describe large features visible on a world map. |
| Old volcanic plains | Each district can feed several generations of flood basalt. With catchment area $S$, 2.5% of $F S$ available for surface lava, rock density 3,000 kg/m³ and cooling enthalpy 1.5 MJ/kg, the erupted volume over time $t$ is $V=0.025 F S t/(3000 \times 1.5 \times 10^6)$. A stack of flows 75–230 m thick gives area $V/d$, capped at 90% of the catchment. The assumed accumulation interval spans 2,800–84,000 years divided by $1+F/20$ to represent faster burial at high flux. A connected flood spreads across the sampled relief, favoring valleys and one rift direction. The covered fraction tapers across the distal part of each field as its lava supply runs out. Interleaved older rock shows through. Younger fields can partially bury several older ones. |
| Flank flows | Each eruptive episode starts on a flank. Its volume budget scales with edifice volume, $V=c r^2 H$, with $c$ between 0.0009 and 0.0027. A 12–36 m thick flow field of width $w$ has nominal runout $V/(w d)$. Width spans 15–59% of edifice radius. Runout is capped at 1,000 km or a quarter planetary radius. Each branch samples lower ground on the bump map, checks the intervening slope and stops at a local minimum or at its budget. Younger fields can bury part of older ones. Width, erupted fraction and cooling thickness are assumptions about eruption history. [USGS flow routing](https://www.usgs.gov/observatories/hvo/science/lava-flow-hazards-zones-and-flow-forecast-methods-island-hawaii) |
| Plumes and fallout | For a ballistic particle launched at angle $\theta$, height is $v^2\sin^2\theta/(2g)$ and range is $v^2\sin(2\theta)/g$. At 45° and 700 m/s, this gives about 68 km height and 272 km range on Io, versus 11 km and 45 km on b. The generator uses the range for coarse fallout, with a scenario speed of 180–1,030 m/s. The range is bounded on small bodies, and the ballistic treatment applies below 100 Pa to particles below escape speed. Dark floors and lighter silicate ash supply contrast. A pale SO₂ deposit is possible where the atmospheric inventory and local temperature permit frost. The saturation approximation is $p_s=1670\exp[4510(1/197.64-1/T)]$ Pa. The map represents accumulated fallout. Plume gas and airborne dust would require transport over time. [Galileo observations of Pele](https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/pele-plume-deposit-on-io/) · [SO₂ phase data](https://webbook.nist.gov/cgi/cbook.cgi?ID=C7446095&Mask=4) |
| Surface brightness | The dry volcanic plains use a nominal display range around sRGB 55–128, roughly 4–22% after sRGB decoding. Fresh flows and pit floors are darker, older deposits lighter. RGB is a relative visible-color proxy. The bolometric Bond albedo used for the climate remains a property of the planet. The host star supplies the daylight tint. |
| Incandescence | The exposed area fraction is $f=\min[0.002,0.10F/(0.95\sigma T^4)]$ for $T=1450$ K. At 100 W/m² that is about 0.0042% of the world. Ten percent of the tidal power, emissivity 0.95 and melt temperature are explicit assumptions. The rest of the heat can escape through cooler material. Active floors share the budget in proportion to their areas. Small openings along their margins are filtered over the texel footprint and tone-mapped so a subpixel source can be seen. A bright pixel can contain mostly solid crust. [Mura et al., Juno lava lakes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10688) |
